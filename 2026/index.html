What is Pace?

Your typical clean lap: half were faster, half slower. Gap is seconds a lap against the middle of the field; minus is faster. Clean is the share of your laps with no cut and no contact. Aim for 50% or more.

How to raise your Pace

1

Make your best lap your typical lap
Best is how much faster your best clean lap was than your Pace. Improving your Rhythm pushes your typical lap towards it.

2

Use the full track width
Go all the way to the white lines on entry, apex and exit. A wider line keeps more speed through the corner. Stay inside the lines: a cut lap does not count.

3

Speed comes out of the corners
Brake a touch earlier and get on the power sooner. A good exit onto a straight is worth more than a late brake.

4

One corner at a time
Pick the corner where you lose most and work on it in practice.

Example

Driver A's best clean lap is 2 s faster than his Pace. That lap already beats the driver ahead of him. Make it his typical lap and he moves up the field.

What is Rhythm?

How steady you were against your own pace, out of 100. Each lap scores 3 points within 0.5% of your pace, 2 within 1% and 1 within 1.5%.

Moments are laps further off, with the seconds they cost. Cuts are the times you left the track.

How to raise your Rhythm

Rhythm rewards repeating your lap, not driving faster. A steady driver at any speed can score 100.

1

Avoid the big moments
An off or a spin scores nothing and costs many seconds. Fewer moments is the quickest way to gain both points and time.

2

Drive a lap you can repeat
Brake at the same marker every lap, a touch early. Give up a tenth to keep the next lap the same.

3

Plan your traffic
Pass slower cars where it is easy. A lap stuck behind one can score nothing, just like a mistake.

4

Keep your laps clean
A lap with a cut or a contact is not clean. Your Pace uses clean laps only: aim for 50% or more. Too few clean laps means no Pace, and without a Pace there is no Rhythm. Warnings also lead to a drive-through.

Example

Driver A and Driver B have the same pace. A has 2 moments that cost 5 s. B has 7 that cost 78 s. Same speed, but the moments cost B more than a minute.
